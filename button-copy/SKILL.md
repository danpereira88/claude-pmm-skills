# Button copy

There is no such thing as microcopy. Customers don't think any words they read are small, and buttons are where conversion actually happens online. They deserve the most care on the page, not the least. Testing buttons is an easy win.

## Call to value vs. call to action

- **Call to value (up the funnel):** the reader is still pursuing value, so the button says what they get, not what they must do.
  - Instead of "Shop now" on a homepage, write "See all the ways to love how you look".
  - "Shop now" just says it's time to go shopping, and it's dangerously close to depleting their assets. The real question is "what do I want?", and the button should answer it.
- **Call to action (point of purchase):** when the credit card comes out, the reader is ready for action and needs to know exactly what happens next.
  - "Add to cart", "Complete purchase", "Start my trial".

Rule: **calls to value further up the funnel, calls to action in checkout.** Never introduce friction.

## Reduce the friction of commitment

A reader who has been burned by other products doesn't want to commit to a whole new system. Lower the stakes in the button: "Give [product] a try" asks for less than a commitment. Hypothesis: lower felt commitment, more conversions.

## Checks for each button

- Would someone feel safe clicking it?
- Are there words you don't need? Can any be swapped out?
- Does it match the reader's stage: value up-funnel, action at purchase?
- Is it clear about what happens next at the point of purchase?
- Does it avoid words that signal losing money?

## Button map audit

1. Map the site (the site map is a good start).
2. For each page, list every button and label it **call to value** or **call to action**.
3. Flag calls to action up-funnel that would work better as calls to value, and unclear calls to action at the point of purchase.
4. Walk the team through it (e.g. a lunch-and-learn) rather than changing everything at once, so everyone aligns on why button copy matters.

## Output

For writing: 3 to 5 options per button, labeled value or action, with the recommended one. For audits: a table of page, current button, type, issue, and suggested rewrite.
