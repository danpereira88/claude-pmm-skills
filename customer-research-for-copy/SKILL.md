# Customer research for copy

Never guess at who you're writing for. Base the message on what customers say, and decide what the copy leads with by where the reader is when they arrive.

## 1. Gather voice-of-customer input

Work from whatever exists: survey answers, reviews, interviews, support tickets, sales call notes, testimonials. Pull out the customer's own words for:
- their problem
- the outcome they want
- their objections
- the moment they decided to act

If no research exists, recommend a light setup, not an exhaustive one:
- Survey only **two groups: new leads and new customers.**
- Always be running a **thank-you page survey.**
- Work with the head of growth to interview **5 to 7** leads or customers, depending on what you're writing.
- Ask the team: Tell me about our customer today. Is our current customer different from our future customer? Where can I see recordings of customers using the product?

## 2. Place the reader on the grid

**Awareness stages**, least to most aware:
- **Unaware:** no felt problem. This is mostly the job of mass advertising and content marketing, not conversion copy. Flag it if the brief targets this reader.
- **Problem aware:** knows the problem, hasn't found a solution, doesn't know any products.
- **Solution aware:** knows solutions exist and may have seen the brand name, but hasn't connected it to their need.
- **Product aware:** knows the product, isn't sold yet.
- **Most aware:** knows the product and the offer. This is where the sale happens most easily.

**Motivation (intent):** low, moderate, or high. High means they're feeling the problem strongly right now, or the offer is so good they have to act.

Plot it as a table: stages across the bottom, motivation down the side. Find the reader's cell by asking concrete questions about the specific person, out of plain curiosity rather than from a fixed checklist. Example, for a product that bounces babies to sleep, aimed at a new dad who takes the day shift:

| Question | If yes | If no |
|---|---|---|
| Does he always sleep through the night? | Low motivation, likely unaware. Not the right prospect for conversion copy | Some motivation, problem aware |
| Did he sleep through the night last night? | Motivation stays low | Definitely problem aware, motivation rising |
| Is he trying to sleep right now? (time of day, search keyword) | High motivation | Moderate |
| Has he heard of our product? | Product aware | Solution aware at best |
| Is this a second visit: a retargeting page or an abandoned cart email? | Most aware, high intent | Late solution aware to early product aware |

Signals to use: time of day and keyword of the search (work with the SEO lead), retargeting, abandoned cart triggers, and brand searches. Work with SEO to build targeted paid landing pages for high-intent times of day and organic landing pages for brand searches.

## 3. What the placement changes

- **The more aware and the higher the intent, the shorter the copy.** The further up the funnel, the longer it needs to be.
- **Most aware, high intent** (e.g. retargeting page, abandoned cart email): the hero joins them where they are. Lead with the offer and the Buy Now button. Don't spend time on the problem, the category, or product features. At most add a quick checklist of reminders that make them feel confident buying now. Skip problem-agitation-solution.
- **Late solution aware to early product aware, moderate to high intent:** don't spend the headline or hero on the problem. Spend a little on solutions and the category, then show why this product is the best solution. Don't lead with the offer.
- **Problem aware or lower intent:** longer copy, with more problem and solution before the product.

## Output

State the reader's cell (stage + motivation), the evidence for it, what the copy should and shouldn't lead with, and the rough length. Include the grid when presenting copy, so anyone who missed the meeting knows why the copy is written the way it is.
