# Sales Pitch (April Dunford) — deriving the pitch from positioning

Read this when the user wants a **sales narrative, pitch deck, demo flow, or landing-page story** — not just the positioning. It assumes a Positioning Canvas already exists (build one first; the pitch is downstream of positioning).

## The core reframe

A sales pitch should answer **"why pick us over the alternatives?"** — not "why pick us?". The most dangerous competitor is almost never another vendor; it is **the customer doing nothing**, because complex purchases are dominated by buyer indecision and fear of choosing wrong. A feature walkthrough makes this worse: it dumps capability without giving the buyer a way to judge between options. The pitch's job is to give the buyer a confident way to decide — and to lead them to the conclusion that your differentiated value is what they need.

## Structure: two phases, eight steps

The pitch splits into **The Setup** (orient the buyer to the market) and **The Follow-Through** (your solution). Differentiated value is the star; everything in the Setup exists to make that value matter.

### The Setup — "the market"

1. **Insight** — your point of view on the market: the thing best-fit customers need to understand for your differentiated value to matter. Often "the problem behind the problem." This is the hardest and most important step. It also doubles as great founder/CEO conference-talk or thought-leadership material.
2. **Alternatives** — survey how buyers solve this today, grouped into a few logical *approaches* (not a vendor-by-vendor list), each with honest pros and cons — including the status quo. This is the natural place for sales discovery: what are you using now, what else have you tried?
3. **The Perfect World** — given those trade-offs, define what the ideal solution would look like. This is really a way of **setting the purchase criteria** — criteria your differentiated value happens to satisfy. Get the buyer to agree on the criteria before you show the product.

### The Follow-Through — "your solution"

4. **Introduction** — introduce the product, using the **market category** from your positioning to set fast context.
5. **Differentiated value** — the heart of the pitch. Present your **value themes** as the answer to the perfect-world criteria, usually carried through a demo that is organized *around the differentiated value* rather than a feature tour.
6. **Proof** — evidence for each value claim: case studies, metrics, customer quotes, references. Keep a library so the rep can match proof to the prospect.
7. **Objections** (optional) — surface and address the predictable unspoken worries: price, switching cost, integration, adoption risk.
8. **The Ask** — a clear, confident next step. (If discovery in step 2 revealed a bad fit, the honest move may be to disqualify rather than push — "calm confidence.")

## Mapping the Canvas → the pitch

Every pitch element should trace back to a positioning slot. Derive, don't invent:

| Pitch step | Comes from the Positioning Canvas |
|---|---|
| Insight | Differentiated value + best-fit customers (what they must believe for your value to matter) |
| Alternatives | Competitive alternatives, grouped into approaches |
| Perfect World | Differentiated value, expressed as purchase criteria |
| Introduction | Market category |
| Differentiated value | Value themes + unique attributes |
| Proof | Evidence for each value theme |
| Objections | Known weaknesses / "why not us" for the chosen frame |
| The Ask | Best-fit qualification (pursue fits, release non-fits) |

## Adapting to format

The same storyboard flexes across media. In a **live sales call**, the Setup is an interactive conversation (discovery lives in step 2). In a **deck**, the Setup gets its own slides because buyers tolerate context in a presentation. On a **landing page**, compress the Setup hard (a few lines for Insight + Alternatives), blend Perfect World into the value section, and place Proof (testimonials, logos, metrics) and Objections (FAQ) where they fall naturally — because a visitor decides in seconds whether the page is for them.

## Principles to preserve

- Nail the differentiated value first; the pitch collapses without it.
- Fight for business where you're a genuine fit; actively decline bad-fit prospects.
- Be the buyer's guide through the decision, not a feature firehose.
- Keep marketing and sales telling the *same* story — both derive from the one canvas, which is exactly why the canvas is captured as structured, shareable slots.
